## Workflow-owned POIs and blessings

[Impl.Req 833](../../../../tarav-py/notes/live/833-workflow-owned-pois-and-blessings-impl-req.md)
landed the workflow-owned POI migration. Producing workflows now define their
concrete POIs over the neutral shared foundation; the old kernel occurrence
marker and parallel POI occurrence/check model were removed.
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb df52568d0076 -->

Blessings became an independent workflow. Blessability is expressed only by
inheriting `BlessablePOI`, so ordinary POIs do not carry a runtime permission
flag. Check POIs and blessability compose by inheritance without taking
ownership away from the producing workflow.
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb ebc34e2f31d2 -->

Archive also moved its existing skipped-remote notice onto shared typed
notification delivery. The operation still returns its artifact; callers see
one warning by default or may explicitly collect the notification.
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb 0feef4955045 -->

Focused verification passed 149 tests, including POI, Checks, Blessings,
References, Navigation, Skills, SKW, Archive, runtime contracts, Breadcrumbs,
and architecture. The broader repository run reached 1,304 passes; remaining
failures were the already disconnected Models surface and sandbox restrictions
on sockets, uv environment creation, cache access, and Git writes.

## Situation-based POI identity and LiteRecord

The discussion revised POI identity after 833: use the concrete class Ship
and a caller-declared situation, with no separate state identity. Blessings
match that situation identity; conservative resurfacing is preferable to
accidentally accepting a new problem under an old blessing.
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb 97313d9f101b -->
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb 47fa1742fa87 -->

Situation data uses a general LiteRecord. Its accepted values include native
types with established serializers, not just JSON primitives; the caller
does not have to manually convert supported dates or Tarav Types.
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb 62644871e7db -->
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb 3e035d6c8d01 -->

The Design invocation produced
[request 834](../../../../tarav-py/notes/live/834-situation-poi-identity-and-lite-records-impl-req.md),
with the rationale also captured in [POI system](../poi-system.md).
No product implementation was performed in this pass.
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb 50364474389c -->

## Caller-declared identity inputs

The POI discussion was generalized into a design pattern: when hashing a
situation or state, the caller supplies its description as a LiteRecord.
Shared mechanics should not bury heuristics for deciding what the situation
means. Simple defaults remain possible; responsibility for the description
stays with the caller. Captured in
[caller-declared identity inputs](../caller-declared-identity-inputs.md).
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb 0c9ad8a7c5d5 -->

## Situation-based POI identity implemented

Request 834 implemented `LiteRecord` and changed identified POIs to derive
their stable identity from the concrete class Ship plus an explicit
caller-declared situation. Evidence is descriptive rather than identifying;
producers include surface revision inputs when changed content must require a
new blessing.
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb a802b1229e1b -->

Collectors retain repeated emissions and address them with an evaluation-local
zero-based index beside the stable situation identity. Blessings ignore that
index and now have only `blessed` and `unblessed` standing.
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb e2966826ee41 -->

The focused affected suite passed 162 tests. The full suite reached 1,342
passes; its 88 failures were confined to the independently disconnected Models
surface. Ruff and diff checks passed.
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb 2ef52a6f6c3e -->

## StateRecord construction and constraints

The identity-inputs pattern was clarified: explicit mechanics do not require
manual state construction on every call. Defaults and typed builders may
supply the record, while an explicit path remains available. Specialized
state types can keep multiple callers consistent. SKW freshness was accepted
as a useful candidate application.
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb 722be810ac9e -->

The common boundary is a StateRecord that is a LiteRecord, from which the
hash is computed. Application-specific interfaces and checks may constrain
that record. Added this clarification to
[caller-declared identity inputs](../caller-declared-identity-inputs.md#explicit-mechanism-flexible-construction).
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb 9d8cca472b54 -->

## SituationCheck implementation design

The discussion coupled detection and POI identity through an optional
SituationCheck: construct the situation first, then evaluate only that snapshot
and produce POIs identifying it. A general Situation suffices; specialized
situation types remain optional. Object-local providers are useful organization,
not a requirement on every check.
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb b8e7b3e7496b -->
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb 36ab4fa78196 -->
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb 221faf7a1160 -->

with a first SKW freshness integration and verification plan. The rationale is
also captured in [Checks](../checks-workflow.md#situationcheck--design).
No product code was implemented in this pass.

## SituationCheck implemented

[Request 835](../../../../tarav-py/notes/live/835-situation-checks-impl-req.md)
implemented immutable Situation snapshots and `SituationCheck`. The evaluated
situation is also the CheckPOI identity input; mismatched results are rejected.
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb b2cde0c2b002 -->

SKW generation freshness and tracked-context freshness are now separate bound
checks because they are independently assessable. Each whole Situation defines
its acceptance scope, and intentionally extra fields may cause conservative
resurfacing rather than reuse an earlier blessing.
<!-- tarav.breadcrumb a4faee02ab22 -->

The maintained foundational suite passes 150 tests in under 13 seconds, and
focused Ruff checks pass. Product completion commit: `0befae4`.
